# Startup Idea Scout 2026-10-11 (cash-back and rewards)

## 0. Bottom line

One idea passed all four company tests, with a borderline platform test, so it is the only one worth pursuing: an **independent attribution-integrity auditor** for advertisers that pay affiliate commissions across several networks. Theme: US shopping cash-back, rewards and affiliate loyalty. I chose it because you asked for ideas closer to cash-back and rewards and confirmed that conflict of interest is not a concern, and because it is the industry your profile names as your market knowledge. The pain is fresh and documented: Honey-style extensions allegedly diverted commissions while hiding from testers [S3], advertisers pay people to hunt attribution manipulation [S4], and networks reinstated the accused extension after it adopted an SDK [S10]. The biggest unknown is the platform test: Impact.com said it would ship its own fix [S15], so the case rests on advertisers running several networks wanting one independent check. Insufficient signal for a second idea: the other five candidates failed on competition, platform or buyer evidence.

## 2. Ideas

### Idea 1: Independent attribution-integrity auditor for affiliate programs (score 25/35)

- **One-liner**: For retailers and brands that pay affiliate commissions across several networks, a continuous, independent audit that tests whether cashback, coupon and AI-shopping publishers stand down and attribute honestly, using realistic synthetic shoppers, and hands back evidence packs for network disputes and clawbacks.
- **Company shape**: wedge (stand-down and cookie-overwrite audits of browser extensions for 10 to 20 multi-network retail advertisers) → expansion (every publisher type including AI-agent traffic, commission clawback workflow, an audit standard that networks and insurers accept) → moat (a library of extension and publisher behaviours and evasion signatures that grows with every advertiser audited, plus an evidence archive usable in disputes).
- **Who pays**: the head of affiliate or partnerships, or the fraud and compliance lead, at a retailer; they already spend on affiliate commissions ($13.63B in the US in 2024 [S8]), on fraud and compliance staff ($75,000 to $85,000 roles [S4]), and on commissions "paid twice for one sale" [S3].
- **Business model**: annual subscription by monitored program, indicative $30,000 a year (estimate), plus an optional success fee on recovered commissions (estimate).
- **Market size**: estimate: 1,500 advertisers with programs large enough to buy × $30,000 a year = $45M a year; about 235 advertisers at that price reach $7M (about ¥1B). Both the buyer count and the price are estimates and unverified; the only sourced input is the spend benchmark of $13.63B with cashback, loyalty and rewards at 35% [S8]. The PMA sample alone cites over 20,000 merchants [S8], so 1,500 large buyers would be 7.5% of them (estimate).
- **Pain evidence**: [S3] extensions allegedly hid from testers and the cost sits in advertiser budgets; [S4] advertisers hire investigators for attribution manipulation; [S1] a rewards company names evolving fraud as a risk; [S7] only 2% of publishers feel very confident in tracking accuracy.
- **Why now**: [S9] AI agents break cookie-based tracking and add a new publisher class to audit; [S10] a draft Code of Conduct and an open SDK now define stand-down rules in machine-readable form, so there is something concrete to test against; Honey's tester-detection (account age, cashback points, prior visits) can only be defeated by synthetic shoppers with realistic histories.
- **Competitors**: [Marcode](https://shyft.ai/tools/marcode) lists extension monitoring but its page is an AI-generated directory entry, so scope is unverified; [Bluepear](https://www.bluepear.net/about-us) covers branded search and affiliate bidding, not extensions; [Impact.com's monitoring](https://help.impact.com/en/support/solutions/articles/155000000156-manage-your-monitoring-violations) covers expired codes, branded keyword bidding and harmful ads, creates no violations for non-contracted partners [S19], and the network said it would ship an attribution fix [S15]; [Rakuten Rewards' SDK](https://ppc.land/rakuten-launches-open-source-sdk-as-honey-returns-to-its-network/) helps extensions comply but does not audit them. No funded head-on company (over $100M) was found. Gap: a cross-network, advertiser-side audit that defeats tester-detection.
- **Platform risk**: Impact.com, Awin and Rakuten Advertising are the platforms. They plausibly add single-network checks (Impact said it would [S15]), so this is borderline. Why they would not ship the idea itself: each sees only its own network, while advertisers running several networks carry the residual exposure [S3]; networks earn on the publishers they police [S3] and reinstated the accused extension after it adopted an SDK [S10]. Speculation: ad-verification firms could extend into affiliate; none was found doing so.
- **Company test**: buyer pass / size pass / moat pass / platform pass
  - Buyer: named role, existing headcount and commission budgets [S4] [S8].
  - Size: path to $7M needs about 235 advertisers at $30,000 (estimate); buyer count unsourced, so this passes only on the estimate.
  - Moat: behaviour and evasion library plus evidence archive grows per audit; "first" alone would not count.
  - Platform: passes only on the multi-network independence argument above; this is the test most likely to flip.
- **Your edge**: the profile says you work on Rakuten's US cash-back business and know US e-commerce and cash-back, and you study verifiers and independent checks. You know the publisher side of stand-down rules. You said conflict of interest is not a concern; the profile rule against using internal data still applies.
- **First validation**: on your own machine, build a test storefront with synthetic data and a mock affiliate network, plus a local extension that simulates Honey-style tester detection (account age, cashback points, prior visit to a network site). Run a fresh-profile tester and a synthetic shopper with history against it. Never point it at a real merchant or network. Then show the result to affiliate managers. **Pass criteria**: the synthetic shopper catches at least one planted evasion that the fresh-profile tester misses; at least 3 of 10 managers describe a real incident with a dollar amount; at least 2 agree to a paid pilot.
- **3 interview questions**: (1) Tell me about the last time you found a publisher or extension taking credit for a sale it did not drive: how did you find out and what did it cost? (2) Who checks this today (people, tools, network reports), and what do you spend on it? (3) When a network told you a publisher was compliant, how did you verify it?
- **Kill criteria**: fewer than 3 of 10 managers recall a real incident; they say their network's monitoring is enough; or a network ships a cross-network or advertiser-side stand-down audit.
- **Score**: pain 4 / why now 4 / market 3 / moat 3 / competition 3 / edge 4 / validation 4

## 3. Killed candidates

- **Agentic-commerce attribution and rewards layer** (credit rewards when AI agents check out): platform fails. Agent platforms control checkout and charge merchants directly (the search reported a 4% fee on OpenAI checkout, unverified), Wildfire has raised about $36M [S14], and Minty is already inside ChatGPT [S17]. It survives only as the expansion of Idea 1 [S9].
- **Cross-network commission reconciliation for publishers**: buyer and size fail on evidence. Publishers are squeezed [S6] and unsure of tracking [S7], but no source shows what they spend on reconciliation, no buyer count was found (estimate: unsourced), and networks can add reconciliation reports. Kept as a lead.
- **Rewards-abuse intelligence consortium for issuers and retailers**: competition and platform fail. Many fraud vendors already sell promo and loyalty abuse detection [S16], and card networks run consortium products. The Citi post shows banks share intelligence by hand [S5], which is a lead, not a gap.
- **Incrementality measurement for cashback and coupon partners**: competition fails. Agencies and networks already sell it [S18], and the headline figures that coupon and cashback activity is largely non-incremental come from vendors with something to sell.
- **Consumer rewards-optimizer agent** (picks the best card, offer and cashback at checkout): buyer and platform fail. No evidence consumers pay, and issuers, Rakuten and extension makers can ship it as a feature [S14] [S17].

## 4. Leads for next time

- Publisher-side commission reconciliation across networks: needs a buyer count and evidence of spend before it can be scored [S6] [S7].
- Rewards-abuse intelligence sharing among issuers: banks describe industry roundtables [S5]; check antitrust, regulation and whether Visa or Mastercard already offers it.
- Retailer insourcing of offers (Walmart can terminate Ibotta on 270 days' notice [S2]): a risk to every rewards middleman, worth a separate look at what retailers build instead.
- Add non-reddit operator voices next time (TopCashback and publisher forums, affiliate trade groups) to test Idea 1's buyer claims.

## 5. For you to decide

- Idea 1 should be validated only in a test environment you control. Do you want to proceed with the local synthetic-shopper demo, and do you want Claude to draft the interview outreach? (Contacting specific people is not done without your approval.)
- Idea 1's biggest uncertainty is whether advertisers care enough to pay a separate vendor; your knowledge of how Rakuten Advertising advertisers react to Honey-style incidents is the fastest check. Share it only if it does not use internal data.
- Do you want a second run on a different cash-back angle (publisher-side reconciliation or issuer-side rewards abuse), or a deeper dive on Idea 1?
