ARCHIVAL RECORD — MUNICIPAL LAMP MAINTENANCE BOARD
Series 14, Volume 3. Transcribed from the original ledgers.
Classification: routine. Retention: permanent.

PREAMBLE

The following record is transcribed from the maintenance ledgers of the
Municipal Lamp Maintenance Board covering the period from the first quarter
of the ninth year through the final inspection of the eleventh. The ledgers
were kept in a consistent hand throughout, and where the original entries
were illegible the transcriber has indicated the gap with a bracketed note.
No attempt has been made to correct the arithmetic of the original clerks,
which is preserved as written even where it does not reconcile.

SECTION ONE — WICK INSPECTIONS

Wick inspections were conducted on a rotating schedule, each lamp receiving
attention no less than once per quarter. The inspector recorded the length
of the exposed wick in sixteenths of an inch, the colour of the flame at the
moment of inspection, and whether the glass required cleaning. Entries from
the early period are terse. A typical entry reads: "Lamp 41, wick nine, flame
steady, glass fouled." Later entries grow more discursive, and by the middle
of the tenth year the inspectors had begun to include remarks on the weather,
the condition of the surrounding pavement, and in several cases the behaviour
of passers-by.

The Board noted in its annual review that this expansion of scope, while not
authorised, had produced a record of some incidental value, and declined to
discourage it.

SECTION TWO — OIL RECEIPTS AND CONSUMPTION

Oil was received in barrels of nominal forty gallons and decanted into the
service cans at the depot. The discrepancy between nominal and actual volume
was a persistent source of correspondence between the Board and its supplier,
and the ledgers record a running tally of shortfalls that by the end of the
tenth year amounted to a figure the Board described as "not trivial."

Consumption was recorded per lamp per week. The seasonal variation is marked:
consumption in the darkest weeks ran to nearly three times the midsummer
figure, which the Board attributed to the obvious cause and did not
investigate further. A minority report filed by one member suggested that
part of the variation was attributable to the inspectors themselves, who in
cold weather were observed to trim the wicks less carefully. This report was
received and filed without action.

SECTION THREE — GLASS AND HOUSING

Breakage was recorded by cause where the cause was known. The categories in
use were: weather, vehicle, deliberate, and unknown. The proportion assigned
to "unknown" declined steadily across the period, which the Board took as
evidence of improving record-keeping rather than of any change in the
underlying pattern of breakage.

Replacement glass was sourced locally until the eleventh year, when a change
of supplier introduced a panel of slightly different dimensions. The ledgers
record a period of some confusion during which both sizes were held in stock
and issued interchangeably, with predictable consequences.

SECTION FOUR — PERSONNEL

The Board employed between four and seven inspectors across the period.
Turnover was highest among those assigned to the outer districts, where the
rounds were longest and the lamps most exposed. The ledgers record the names
of the inspectors but not their wages, which were kept in a separate book now
lost.
